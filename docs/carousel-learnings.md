# Learnings · carousels in general

Criteria that apply to **any project**, learned in pilots and reviews. What belongs to a single brand goes in `projects/<slug>/learnings.md`.

Format: the criterion, where it came from and how to apply it. New entries at the end of each section. The criteria below came from the reviews of the original process; the origin was summarized so as not to identify clients.

---

## Image and meaning

1. **The image tells the same stage as the text.** If the text talks about pregnancy, the photo shows a pregnant woman, not a mother with a baby. Don't anticipate phases the text hasn't reached yet.
   *Origin: pilot review.*
2. **The cover represents the topic directly.** Show the subject and who it involves (e.g.: "vaccine during pregnancy" → pregnant woman being vaccinated, or a clear vaccine element). A generic or merely atmospheric photo (silhouette, artistic B&W) doesn't work for an informative topic.
   *Origin: pilot review.*
3. **The photos follow an arc.** The sequence follows the story of the text, and the ending can show the result (e.g.: the baby on the last slide, after talking about pregnancy).
   *Origin: pilot review.*
4. **Order of criteria when choosing the photo:**
   1. meaning (criteria 1 to 3);
   2. non-negotiables (face, minimum contrast, seam);
   3. recommended contrast, format and uses.

   High contrast doesn't make up for a photo that doesn't tell the subject.
   *Origin: pilot review.*
5. **When the bank doesn't have the right photo, that is a finding.** The review board records the gap (e.g.: "missing a pregnant woman being vaccinated") as a shot-list item to produce or buy the photo. Don't fill it with a photo that only "gets by".

6. **A photo with a mark of its time loses.** A Covid mask and other dated signs make the photo look old; it loses to another that tells the same stage of the text.
   *Origin: pilot review.*

7. **The photo tells the story of the reader, not of the one providing care.** In a patient topic (diagnosis, exam, appointment), the focus of the photo is the patient and what happens to her, not the doctor. A doctor facing the camera and the patient from behind, or only the patient's hand, loses to a photo in which the patient appears.
   *Origin: pilot review.*
8. **On the closing slide, the visible half needs to make sense on its own.** The panel covers the right half, so the photo is framed for the left half: the main person fully there, without cutting people in half.
   *Origin: pilot review.*
9. **One carousel, one color tone.** All the photos in the same carousel need to match in temperature, brightness and saturation; a cold, light photo in the middle of warm, dark photos breaks the art direction, even if each one, on its own, is right. In B3, compare the photos side by side (temperature = R−B, average brightness and saturation) and, in B4, apply a light treatment (temperature, exposure, saturation) to the ones that are off. If the light treatment isn't enough, the photo loses.
   *Origin: pilot review.*
10. **The people look like the brand's audience.** An external photo (Pexels, Freepik) tends to show people from outside Brazil; before accepting, check whether the person could be a Brazilian patient. Search with terms like "brazilian", "latina" and check the preview; when in doubt, it loses.
   *Origin: pilot review.*
11. **In the spread, the person appears on the slide where the text talks about them and continues on the next one.** If 02 talks about who receives the diagnosis, she starts on 02 and continues on 03, rather than being entirely on 03 with 02 empty. Mirroring the photo is a valid resource (no text or brand in the image); the face stays away from the seam.
   *Origin: pilot review.*

## Carousel structure

1. **One spread may be enough.** The rule asks for at least one, not one per pair of slides. The last slide can have its own photo, without sharing with the previous one.
   *Origin: pilot review.*
2. **The carousel has the slides the client sent, no more, no less.** A slide the request doesn't include (a signature, for example) isn't added, even if the design system has the component.
   *Origin: pilot review.*
3. **The request's "+++" row doesn't go in.** It belongs to the client's table template, not to the carousel, even when it comes with text. The script already ignores it; don't rescue it by hand.
   *Origin: pilot review.*
4. **The client's bold stays bold**, on any slide with text (content or closing slide). If the component has nowhere to put it, the review board decides how to show it; the sentence doesn't blend into the paragraph.
   *Origin: pilot review.*

## Assembly and check

1. **Text can't be cut by a mask.** Descenders (g, j, ç, p) of the italic highlight go past the text box. Frames with text don't clip content; only the slide clips. Check it in the screenshot.
   *Origin: pilot review.*
2. **The contrast measurement doesn't see faces or seams.** The review board screenshot is mandatory before the review.
   *Origin: pilot review.*

3. **A face needs breathing room, not just "not touching".** On the cover and the content slides, the title and the text stay away from the face. First try to **reframe** (shift or zoom the photo) or **break the title into more lines**; only then change the text position or the photo. Check the cover too: in a pilot the title fell over the face and got through the check.
   *Origin: pilot review.*
4. **Zoom to clean up the scene.** When the photo has things that don't add (equipment, floor, edge of an object), zoom and reframe before accepting. The spread uses the same crop on both halves.
   *Origin: pilot review.*
5. **Cover title in up to 3 short lines**, broken by hand when the automatic break leaves a long line or pushes the title over the face.
   *Origin: pilot review.*
6. **Widow in a narrow column: join 2 words, not 3.** In narrow text boxes (~380 px with 36 px text), three words joined by a non-breaking space can go past the width, and Figma breaks the word in the middle ("crian-ças"). In these cases, join only the last 2 and check in the screenshot.
   *Origin: pilot review.*

## Review board

1. **Whoever reviews looks, doesn't read.** What needs a decision has to show up in the image itself or in a short, visible notice. Alternative photos and long reasons are support, not the main thing.
   *Origin: pilot review.*
